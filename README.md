Background “Traditionally, doctors prescribe standard drug doses for everyone. But we know that people respond differently to the same medication. Some metabolize drugs too quickly, others too slowly, and this can lead to reduced effectiveness or even dangerous side effects. Precision Posology is an approach that determines the exact drug dosage that will be most effective and safe for an individual patient. It uses bioinformatics and genetic data to tailor treatment, ensuring better outcomes and fewer adverse drug reactions.” Aim “The aim of our study is to develop and evaluate a computational framework that integrates patient-specific genomic data with the Pharmacogenetics Knowledgebase, or PharmGKB. By doing this, we can transition from generalized drug dosing to precision dosing, optimizing therapeutic efficacy while minimizing adverse drug reactions.” Objectives “Our objectives are clear: - To move away from the generalized ‘one-size-fits-all’ approach. - To optimize drug selection and dosage based on an individual’s genetic profile. - To explore how bioinformatics pipelines, combined with PharmGKB, can translate complex genomic data into actionable clinical prescriptions. In short, we want to show how computational biology can directly improve patient care.” Methodology “Here’s how we approached the study: - Using high-throughput sequencing technologies, we identify patient-specific genomic variants. - These variants are mapped to PharmGKB clinical annotations to infer metabolic phenotypes — basically, how a person’s body processes drugs. - This classification allows us to adjust dosages precisely and select the right drug, reducing the risk of adverse drug reactions. - We also examine algorithmic frameworks that integrate polygenic risk scores with clinical variables like age, body weight, and comorbidities. This makes predictions more accurate and clinically relevant.” Bar Graph – Drug Metabolism Rate (Top/Bottom Left) “This bar graph shows how different drugs are metabolized in the body. On the x-axis, we have different drugs, and on the y-axis, we have the metabolism rate. We can see that some drugs are metabolized faster, while others are slower. This is important because if a drug is metabolized too quickly, it may not work effectively. If it is too slow, it may build up in the body and cause side effects. So, this graph shows why dosage needs to be adjusted for each patient.” Multi-color Bar Comparison (Top Left small graph) “This graph compares drug metabolism under different conditions or for different individuals. The different colored bars represent variations between patients. This highlights that people process the same drug differently, mainly due to genetic differences. That’s why a ‘one-size-fits-all’ dose does not work for everyone.” Heatmap – Drug Metabolism vs Enzymes (Bottom Right) “This heatmap shows how different enzymes affect drug metabolism. Each row represents a specific enzyme, and each column represents drug metabolism levels. The colors indicate the rate — brighter colors mean higher metabolism, and darker colors mean lower metabolism. This helps us understand which enzymes are more active in breaking down certain drugs. Since enzyme activity is influenced by genetics, this helps in predicting how a patient will respond to a drug.” “Overall, these graphs show that drug response varies from person to person, and this is why Precision Posology is needed to choose the right drug and dose for each individual.” 📊 Results / Concept Script: “Our concept supports a major shift in medicine — from reactive to predictive. Instead of waiting for a patient to have a bad reaction, we can use data to proactively decide the safest and most effective drug and dosage. This multidimensional approach enables proactive, data-driven therapeutic decisions.”
